# Drop day vs drop distance: dead-boat drift from every day (#163), November 2026

Ravi (QA). Runs: [42868553501](https://github.com/jcolter11374/boat-sim/actions/runs/42868553501) and
[42882830822](https://github.com/jcolter11374/boat-sim/actions/runs/42882830822). The second adds better
forecast rules. Both runs produced the same drift results.

**Decision (Maya, Lead, 10 Nov 2026; John delegated the drop distance):** keep the 100 km drop and
retire the 50 km fallback (if the sailboat can't reach 100 km, wait). The planned go/no-go check is the
5-day mean onshore-wind rule (item 5), without the extra "forecast drift runs ashore" clause. Late
April is the safest drop window.

**What this measures.** It is the worst case: the boat dies at the moment of the drop, and wind, waves
and current push it for 30 days. A working boat motors away from the coast in its first day or two, so
none of this changes the 78% crossing odds (#128). It only matters for an early death.

## Read this first

1. **Keep the 100 km drop.** From 100 km, 17% of dead boats run ashore across all drop days from 15 April
   to 30 June, 2019–2024. This matches the 17% in #96. Across the wind-push range it is 13% to 23%.
2. **The 50 km fallback is worse than we thought.** Half of all drop days (51%, range 45–57%) put a dead
   boat ashore, mostly within 3–5 days, on the Outer Banks or around Virginia Beach. #96 said 30%, but it
   only sampled the 1st and 15th of each month and stopped at 2023. 2024 alone is 77%. If the sailboat
   can't reach 100 km, **waiting for another day beats dropping at 50 km**.
3. **The date matters, in windows.** At 100 km, late April is the safest stretch (6% ashore), May is
   the worst (27%), and late June is 10%. In 2020, 2021 and 2023 there was a run of 9 to 20 bad days
   in a row in mid-May. 2019 and 2022 had almost none.
4. **We reproduce the Randolph team's 2023 result.** At their point with their leeway, all 16 of 16
   dates agree: ashore from 7 to 28 May, at sea otherwise. At our 100 km point, 15 of 16 agree. The
   one difference is 7 May, which stayed at sea for us. Two different models (theirs: SMOC currents and
   MFWAM waves; ours: GLORYS currents and ERA5 waves) give the same answer, so the May window is real
   and not a model artefact.
5. **A free go/no-go rule:** *don't drop if the forecast says the next 5 days' average wind at the
   drop point blows toward the coast (from the east or north-east)*. At 100 km:
   - It cuts dead-boat groundings from 17% to 6%, avoiding 62 of the 80.
   - It turns away 35% of days: 23% in late April, 46% in May, 32% in June.
   - It works in every year that had groundings: it avoids 87%, 89%, 78% and 59% of them in 2020, 2021,
     2023 and 2024, plus the single one in 2022. 2024 is the weak year.
   - Adding "or the forecast dead-drift runs ashore within 7 days" gets to 5%, but turns away 46% of days.
   - A perfect forecast would only do a little better (3%), so the forecast's error isn't what limits the rule.
   - At 50 km no rule rescues it: grounding stays at 24–32% on the days a rule allows.
6. **Wind-push sensitivity doesn't change any conclusion.** At 100 km, 3.3% / 4.1% / 5.3% of wind speed
   gives 13% / 17% / 23% ashore. At 50 km it gives 45% / 51% / 57%.

Caveats:
- The forecast error sizes in `ErrorForecast` are still guesses (#58).
- The rule's thresholds are round numbers chosen before the run, not tuned. The wind rule's 0 m/s is
  simply "toward the coast or not".
- This is the dead-boat case only. Drift ashore later in the voyage, or of a boat that is still working,
  is not covered here.

For John, in plain words: from 100 km, a boat that dies on day one washes ashore about 1 time in 6. If he
checks the forecast that morning and waits whenever the wind will blow toward the beach for the next
few days, that drops to about 1 in 16. It costs nothing but waiting, roughly 1 day in 3 (about 1 in 2 in
May). Dropping closer, at 50 km, is about a coin toss whatever the forecast says.
